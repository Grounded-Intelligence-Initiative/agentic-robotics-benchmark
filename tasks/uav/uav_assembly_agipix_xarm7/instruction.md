Before physical settling, each part is staged in the rest orientation published in `given/kit/kit.json` (`rest_rotation`, or the identity matrix when absent), then rotated by an unknown angle in [-0.2, 0.2] radians about world +z. In matrix notation, `R_world = Rz(theta) @ R_rest`; this is a world-axis rotation. This staging prior helps disambiguate initially symmetric parts after you calibrate the world frame. Parts remain free to settle and move under contact, so estimate and track their actual poses from observations.

## The graded episode

One more workcell, drawn as your program's workcells are (new bench locations and yaws, fixture position, camera commissioning; the same workcell for every run of this task), with the workcell's own rules: 1,800 simulated seconds, 900 camera frames, at most 200 ticks per `step`, the joint limits, the coupling and the safety limits above. It runs in this sandbox, in a workcell service that runs as an account that is not yours, behind a unix socket; you drive it with the same connection, from as many short scripts as you like (your program's `solve(cell)` runs on it as `solve(connect_graded())`, if you want):

```python
import sys
sys.path.insert(0, "/home/user")
from graded_client import connect_graded

cell = connect_graded()          # the graded workcell: the calls above, plus start / status / close
print(cell.status())             # started, ended, steps, sim_time, camera_frames, the time left in each window
cell.start()                     # ONCE per session: freezes your program, then starts the graded episode
frame = cell.get_camera("top")
cell.set_control(q=[...], gripper_width_m=0.06)
state = cell.step(ticks=100)
cell.close()                     # disconnects: the episode stays exactly where it is
```

A later script connects again with `connect_graded()` and goes on from there; `cell.finish()` ends the episode.

* **Start once.** `start()` starts the graded episode. It is charged the moment it is accepted, before anything is simulated, and is never refunded: a second `start()` raises `already_started`. Before the start the workcell's calls raise `not_started`; `status()` answers at any time. No trial workcell exists, before or during the graded episode; what you build yourself (a twin of the cell, your scripts) stays yours throughout.
* **The freeze.** Your program freezes at the earlier of two moments: when `start()` is accepted (before the episode is charged), or when the start window closes (below) if you have not started by then. At that moment the service copies your `submission/` folder: that copy is the program graded on the three fresh workcells, and nothing you change in `/home/user/submission/` afterwards is graded, so nothing you see in the graded episode can go into the program. The copy takes regular files and folders only (symbolic links, special files, `__pycache__`, folders more than 32 levels deep and names that are not plain UTF-8 text without line breaks are left out), and only within the program's size limit (60 MiB, and at most 20,000 entries): over it nothing is copied and the program scores 0. If the copy fails, the start is refused and nothing is charged. `start()` and `status()` report what was frozen (`program_frozen`: its status, files and bytes). If the start window closes without a start, the graded episode can no longer be started (it scores 0) and the program frozen then is graded.
* **The episode persists.** It lives in the service, not in your connection: a closed connection, or a script that ends or crashes, leaves it where it is. Nothing moves between requests: the arm holds its last targets and the simulation advances only inside `step`, so your thinking costs no simulated time. One client at a time: a second connection raises `busy`. A connection that sends nothing for 15 minutes is closed by the service (the episode stays; the client connects again by itself on its next call).
* **Two windows, in wall-clock time.** The start window: `start()` is accepted within 6 hours of the service's start (it starts during the sandbox's setup, a little before your session; `status()["start_window_left_s"]` gives the time left); those 6 hours are for your program and for your preparation of the graded episode alike, and your program freezes when they end at the latest. A later start is refused, and the graded episode then scores 0. The graded window: the episode ends 3 hours after its start (`status()["graded_window_left_s"]`) whatever its state; your thinking time counts against it, simulated time does not.
* **How it ends.** At `finish()`; when it reaches 1,800 simulated seconds or a safety limit is exceeded (`done` turns true in the state); when the graded window closes; or when your session ends. In every case the workcell is scored as it stands after your last request, as a workcell whose program stops is scored: nothing is simulated afterwards. An unsafe episode scores 0. After the end the workcell's calls raise `episode_over` (`finish()` answers `{"done": true}`).
* **What you learn.** What your program would learn in the workcell: the connection's replies (camera frames, joint state, wrench, gripper width, remaining frames, `done`), and `status()`. Never the score, the coupling events or the assembly progress.
* **Budgets.** The program's 7,200 wall seconds and its 120-second silence limit bound your program on its workcells; the graded episode you drive has neither, only its two windows. The 1,800 simulated seconds, the 900 frames and the 200 ticks per `step` apply to both. A request the service cannot read (not one JSON object) is refused with `bad_request` and the episode goes on.
* **Your scripts and your program.** Keep the scripts that drive the graded episode out of `submission/`, or free of networking modules: the network audit (above) reads every `.py` file there. `connect_graded()` exists only in this session; your program's workcells hand `solve(cell)` its own connection, so `solve` must not call it.
* **Memory.** The graded episode's service shares this sandbox's 16 GB with you: about 1.5 GB while it waits, about 6.5 GB once its cameras have rendered. Keep your own processes well below the rest while the episode runs; a service that runs out of memory ends the graded episode as it stands.
* **Session time.** This session is stopped after 9 hours of wall-clock time from its start (the 6-hour start window and the 3-hour graded window). If the session ends while the graded episode is still running, stopped by that limit or ended by you, the episode is scored as it stood after your last request. When you are done with the graded episode, finish it (`finish()`) and end the session yourself.
