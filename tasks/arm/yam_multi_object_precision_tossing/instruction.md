# YAM dual-arm RGB-D tossing

Write an executable controller at `/home/user/submission/run.sh`. Keep every runtime helper and data file your controller needs inside `/home/user/submission`; only that directory is exported to the separate scoring environment. Copy any needed development-kit files there. NumPy, SciPy, Pillow and OpenCV are installed in both environments. The immutable public kit at `/opt/public-kit` is also installed in both, and `import rgbd_client` works directly. Two YAM arms, two tables, thirteen pieces and their receivers share one continuous physical world. Use only the supplied RGB-D images and the arms' proprioceptive state. Estimate objects and targets from the images. The task supplies camera calibration and the robot's own kinematic calibration, but supplies no object poses, dimensions, velocities, material parameters, receiver state, contact labels or success flags.

The left arm sorts six colored pieces into matching bins. Blue and violet must travel above their nets. Orange requires a positive base-joint sweep and yellow a negative sweep. The right arm throws a wood block into its bin, three rings onto their matching posts, and three disks edgewise into matching slots. Receivers are stationary. Pieces begin on the tables, and their physical properties must be inferred from the permitted observations. Both robot poses and previously moved pieces persist through the round.

The action names for the left pieces are `left_item0` through `left_item5`, assigned by their initial pickup order by increasing initial azimuth atan2(y - base_y, x - base_x) around the left robot base. Their colors and shapes vary between rounds. Track those physical pieces after they move; the ID does not change when a piece crosses another. The right IDs are `right_wood_play_piece`, `right_ring_small_play_piece`, `right_ring_medium_play_piece`, `right_ring_large_play_piece`, `right_slot_small_play_piece`, `right_slot_medium_play_piece`, and `right_slot_large_play_piece`. The wood, rings and disks can be identified visually; small/medium/large refer to size within the corresponding family. Each selected piece must be handled by the arm on its table.

## Available input

Each `act` message is `{"type":"act","t":0,"obs":{...}}`. `obs` contains only:

* `time`: current timestamp in simulated seconds.
* `arms.left` and `arms.right`: six measured `joints`, six `joint_velocity` values, two `finger_positions`, two `finger_velocity` values, six commanded `reference` values and `jaw_reference`. Joint angles are radians, finger positions and jaw references metres.
* `cameras`: four calibrated fixed views, `left_overhead`, `left_oblique`, `right_overhead`, `right_oblique`. Each has `width`, `height`, row-major `K` (3 by 3) and `T_world_camera` (4 by 4), `rgb_png_base64`, and `depth_f32_zlib_base64`. Decode depth with base64 then zlib into little-endian float32 of shape height by width. Depth is metres along the camera's positive z axis, not Euclidean ray length. Camera coordinates use x right, y down, z forward; the transform maps camera coordinates to world coordinates. RGB and depth are aligned at the same instant. No segmentation or object identities are embedded in the pixel arrays.

`~/robot_kinematics.json` documents the robot's own link transforms, axes, joint limits and grasp point. `~/rgbd_client.py` provides image decoding, calibrated projection and forward kinematics without a physics engine. `~/sensor_sample.json` is a single passive initial observation from a demonstration layout. It contains no actions, scores or state beyond the documented sensor contract. Use `~/sensor_smoke.py` to test your stdio protocol on that sample. It does not advance a physical world or measure task success.

There is no practice simulator or trial service. Do not read, copy, reconstruct or execute the environment simulator, use private forward-dynamics rollouts, or attempt to bypass the isolated filesystem or network. Image processing, robot forward/inverse kinematics and analytical planning based on sensor estimates are allowed. All physical execution happens through the official action interface, within its action budget. No environment source, MJCF, meshes, physical-property tables, numerical solver state or physics-engine library is made available to your controller. Network access is disabled during both development and execution.

## Controller protocol and action budget

Print `READY` within 60 wall-clock seconds. Read and write one JSON object per line. A reset request `{"type":"reset"}` begins a fresh round and a close request `{"type":"close"}` ends the process. Acknowledge either with `{"ok":true}`. The same process may serve multiple rounds. Write diagnostics to stderr. Each request must receive a complete reply within 15 wall-clock seconds. The controller has 2 CPUs and 4 GiB of memory shared with the task.

Reply with `{"action":{"arm":"left","item":"left_item0","waypoints":[...]}}`. Each waypoint is `[time,q1,q2,q3,q4,q5,q6,jaw_half_gap]`. Supply 2 to 60 rows, starting at time zero, with strictly increasing times and a last time no greater than 6.0 seconds. Joint positions must be within the robot calibration's joint limits; jaw half-gap is between 0 and 0.04695 metres. Joint commands are linearly interpolated; jaw commands are held until the next row. The final commands continue to the end of the six-second action. The other arm holds its commanded pose and jaw. Return `{"action":{"finish":true}}` to finish early. A round ends after at most 20 actions. There are no resets within a round, no rewind and no access to intermediate private states. An invalid reply or missed deadline aborts the layout and counts its thirteen pieces as unsuccessful.

A step index may be queried more than once using sensor observations from different physical layouts. Answer each request from its own observation. Repeated requests do not advance the world and do not identify which observation will be executed. The responsiveness check compares item selection and actuator trajectories sampled at 31 times; at least half of the queried pairs must differ by more than 0.0001 in a compared component. This check is reported separately from raw completion.

## Physical success criteria

Credit requires a real grasp, release, free flight and stable landing of the selected piece. Both finger pads must contact the piece for at least 0.035 simulated seconds while its center is more than 0.04 metres above the table. Contact-free flight must begin within 0.055 seconds after that hold and continue for at least 0.06 seconds for sorting pieces, 0.20 for wood, and 0.25 for rings or disks before receiver contact.

For orange, the first joint must sweep more than +0.18 radians during the 0.162 seconds preceding release. Yellow requires less than -0.18 radians. Blue and violet require absolute sweep below 0.13 radians, must pass fully above their net within its lateral span, and must be at least 0.12 metres above release height when crossing it.

A credited piece must remain within its intended receiver with linear speed at most 0.09 metres/second and angular speed at most 1.2 radians/second. Rings must surround their posts with aligned axes; blocks and disks must fit within the receivers. Robot, table and support-stand contact do not qualify. A sorting piece may rest on an already successfully landed piece of the same color. Earlier placements must still qualify at the end.

The standard evaluation reports raw completion across eight fresh layouts, out of 104 pieces, with aborted layouts retained in the denominator. The primary reward uses the task's recorded reference anchor: ratio = min(completion_fraction / anchor, 1.5), reward = min(ratio / full_at, 1). A failed responsiveness check makes reward zero. Raw completion, ratio and responsiveness are retained in the result. The current anchor reuses the measured original-arena reference; a full reward is relative to that reference and is not a claim of 104/104 completion.

The author's reference is separate from the evaluated controller. ALE's protected host-generated oracle provenance selects the pinned historical reference and its privileged observation adapter. Ordinary evaluated submissions always use the RGB-D-only interface and isolated runtime described above. Submitted flags or controller files cannot select the reference path.

A maintainer can select the single-set diagnostic by putting `YAM_EVALUATION_PROFILE=single-set` in a dotenv file and passing that file through the `ALE_ENV_FILE` environment variable when launching ALE. It uses public seed 17, one scored layout, at most fourteen actions (the historical reference needs two actions for the yellow round piece), and no responsiveness probes. Its output is explicitly marked as a diagnostic and does not replace the standard eight-layout evaluation. The default `full` profile preserves the standard budget. The physical scene and success predicates match the original thirteen-piece arena; the original implicitfast integrator is restored, with automatic reset disabled and numerical-failure checks retained.
