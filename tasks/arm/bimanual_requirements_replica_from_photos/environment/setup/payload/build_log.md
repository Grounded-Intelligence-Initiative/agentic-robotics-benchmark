# Build log of the reference pair (colleague's notes)

[BL-01] Structure. The arm links, yokes and brackets are machined 6082 aluminium, anodised; the column is a 60 x 60 mm
aluminium extrusion on a steel base plate; the finger pads are moulded rubber. Bearings, shafts and fasteners are steel.

[BL-02] Weight. Before fitting the modules I weighed each arm's machined structure (everything that turns with J1:
links, yokes, brackets, bearings, fasteners and the gripper without its module) on the parcel scale: 2.024 kg
(left) and 2.024 kg (right), +-5 g; the gripper sub-assembly alone (fingers, gear pair, shafts, base, no module)
weighed 0.429 kg. I did not weigh the parts one by one.

[BL-03] Harness. Measured 0.4 V drop between the supply terminals and the left J2 module's connector during a fast
move; assume the same at every module.

[BL-04] Cameras. Photos from my phone: the main camera (a wide lens class) and the 2x camera (tele); the class of every photo
is in photos/photos.json. PH-01..PH-12 are from three tripod stations T1 (front-left), T2 (right side) and T3 (high,
front-right) (PH-01 / PH-04 / PH-07 / PH-10 from T1, PH-02 / PH-05 / PH-08 / PH-11 from T2, PH-03 / PH-06 / PH-09 / PH-12 from T3); the tripods stood still while I moved the pair through four
poses P1..P4 (PH-01..PH-03 = P1, PH-04..PH-06 = P2, PH-07..PH-09 = P3, PH-10..PH-12 = P4), and the pair stood still while the three photos of a pose
were taken. The phone refocuses for every photo. Everything else is hand-held.

[BL-05] Stops. PH-13..PH-26: each joint driven slowly into its programmed limit on both arms at once, lower and upper, the
other joints at whatever pose they were in; photos.json names the joint and the stop. PH-27, PH-28, PH-29 and PH-30: the grippers (open stop,
closed, side view). PH-31, PH-32, PH-33, PH-34, PH-35 and PH-36: the module pockets. Module fit as I assembled it: the J1, J2, J3, J5, J6, J7 and gripper modules' housings sit directly in their seats; the J4 module of each arm sits between the elbow yoke's cheeks on a machined plate bolted to the rear cheek, which fills the yoke behind its housing.

[BL-06] Things of known size. The pair's base plate is clamped on the oak bench top; the bench top is the mounting plane.
I laid a 300 mm steel rule (1 / 5 / 10 mm ticks) on the bench for PH-01 and PH-37 only, an ID-1 card (85.60 x 53.98 mm) for
PH-27 only, and stood a beverage can (66 mm diameter, 122 mm tall) on the bench for PH-05 and PH-38 only; the kitchen scale
with a 150 mm steel rule in front of it is only in PH-40. The module housings are the vendor's (outline drawings on
the datasheet pages).

[BL-07] Orientation. The white arrow sticker on the bench in front of the column marks +x (the pair's front).

[BL-08] Modules and lots. The pair ran on the vendor's previous module generation, in the same housings; the new modules
go into the same pockets. Varden Robotics incoming-inspection reports in lot_reports/: LR-2026-0411 (lot VR-2026-W23) and LR-2025-1311 (lot VR-2025-W48).
Ostgard Industrial Drives parts were never inspected here.

[BL-09] Tray. PH-38: the customer's tray with its two handle bars, loaded, next to the can; PH-40: the loaded tray
on our kitchen scale (1 g display resolution, tared) with the 150 mm rule in front of it.

[BL-10] Repair (2026-05-03). The inboard elbow bracket of both arms cracked at a screw boss after a crash; I had them remade in stainless steel to the same drawing, with lightening pockets (the bare, unanodised steel parts in the photos). Each new inboard elbow bracket weighed 2.88 x the original one on the lab scale. BL-02 is the weight of the original build.

[BL-11] Forearm revision. The assembly drawing (rev A, 2025-11-03) dimensions the forearm at 216.0 mm between the elbow point and the wrist centre. On 2026-02-14 I fitted longer forearm tubes on both arms (rev B, for the reach an earlier project needed); the drawing was never updated. The pair in the photos has the rev B forearms, and the weights in BL-02 are with them.

[BL-12] Column. I cut the column so that the pair could work on the bench, and clamped the base plate at the bench's back edge (PH-41).
