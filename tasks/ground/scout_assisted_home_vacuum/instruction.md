# Scout-assisted vacuum in an unfamiliar home

Write a controller for a ground vacuum and a faster indoor survey drone. Clean two spills in each of the two rooms named in the work order, and the entrance-hall spill hidden under a supply carton. Deliver the carton to a work mat inside one of the rooms, then return it to the other mat in the hallway. The work order names the colors in sequence. Keep the brush off on delicate runners and return the vacuum to its dock. You may interleave cleaning, deliveries and exploration. Room connections, orientation, furniture, spills, mats and starting side vary. Some rooms can only be entered through a neighbouring room; the home stays fixed during one run.

Submit an executable `/home/user/submission/run.sh` and any helpers there. It must print `READY` and flush stdout, then process one JSON object per line. Reply to `{"type":"reset"}` and `{"type":"close"}` with `{"ok":true}`; forget all episode state on reset. For `{"type":"act","t":0,"obs":{...}}` return e.g. `{"action":["E","N",2,true]}`. Log only to stderr. Python, Pillow, NumPy and MuJoCo are installed in `/opt/venv`. You may coordinate both robots in one program and build your own map.

**What you get:** `instruction`; `ground` and `air` (base64 PNG, RGB, 84 by 84); `moved` (the counts of cell moves actually completed on each robot's previous command, initially zero); `left` (shared ticks remaining); and `flight` (drone ticks remaining). The two robots start together at the dock. There are no coordinates, room boundaries, top-down house map or online scores on the wire.

The onboard cameras are stabilized, north-up, rectified downward views: 3 by 3 cells for the vacuum and 7 by 7 for the scout. North is image up. Black is unobserved. Walls occlude both cameras; furniture also occludes the vacuum. Each robot's body is masked. Approximate diffuse floor swatches (sRGB hex): hall C5BBA5, oak BC936B, sage 98AD95, slate 8B9EAC, rose C2A2A4. Natural grain, lighting and shadows change individual pixels. Walls have grey caps (656577); furniture has varied materials and dark footings. Dust 684127, carton E4AC29, mats CB4D5D (red) / 397ECB (blue), dock 24A99C, and woven runners 945078 with light stripes. Objects can hide spills or mats. The simulator renders 3D meshes with discrete motion primitives; it does not test flight stabilization. Sample observations from a separate public home are in `/home/user/practice/cameras.jsonl`; they are examples, not a live view of the test homes.

**Actions:** `[vacuum_direction, scout_direction, scout_stride, suction_on]`. Directions are `N/E/S/W/stay`, stride is 0, 1 or 2, and suction is a JSON boolean. Each request advances **one shared tick**. The ground robot attempts one cell move. Entering the carton pushes it one cell in the same direction if the space beyond is clear; it cannot pull. The brush acts on the vacuum's final cell even when stationary: it removes dust, but running it on a woven runner leaves permanent damage. Crossing with the brush off is fine. The scout can pass above the carton and furniture but not through walls. Its first 36 ticks elapse even when hovering; afterward `air` is null. Both actions execute concurrently. Each home allows 200 shared ticks, including blocked moves.

**Scoring:** Each home awards 45% for the mean fraction of dirt physically removed across the two requested rooms and the hall; 12.5% for delivering the carton to its first mat; 12.5% for leaving it on the hall mat *after* the first delivery; and 30% for completing all work, docking and leaving all woven runners undamaged. Parking on the hall mat alone earns no delivery credit. Entering the first mat with the carton is sufficient; there is no wait or confirmation action. Full completion ends the episode. An aborted episode receives zero. The grader runs the controller on six private layouts and averages their scores. A written route, map or self-reported result is not a submission.
