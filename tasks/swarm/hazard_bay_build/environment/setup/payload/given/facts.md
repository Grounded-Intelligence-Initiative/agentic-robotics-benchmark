# Measured facts

What a tape measure and a kitchen scale gave, and what the nameplates say. Nothing here can be read from a picture.

- **The calibration mat**: 7 × 5 squares of 100.0 mm; centre at x = 0.00 m, y = -0.60 m; long side along x.
- **The floor**: sealed concrete; for the simulation, friction 0.5 static and dynamic, no restitution.
- **The six parts on the tables** (kitchen scale): cracker box 411 g, mustard bottle 603 g, sugar box 514 g,
  tomato soup can 349 g, power drill 895 g, mug 118 g.
- **The bucket**: 1.2 kg empty; 10.0 L of water fill it to 3 cm below the rim, and it is full. Three lifting rings
  on the rim, 120° apart, standing 2 cm off the shell.
- **The ropes**: three, one on each ring, 10 mm wire rope, 0.30 kg/m; 1.50 m from the ring to the hook at the far
  end. In the photographs they lie on the floor beside the bucket.
- **The valve** (nameplate): quarter-turn; breakaway torque 4 N·m at the lever.
- **The pressure gauge** (nameplate): linear, 0 bar at its lowest tick, the needle turning clockwise (seen from the
  front) as the pressure rises, 21.875° per bar; the photographs show it at the line's working pressure, 8 bar.
- **The photographs**: `photos/cameras.json` — per photograph the pinhole intrinsics (focal length and sensor
  apertures in mm, image size in pixels), no distortion.
- **The rig**: the plant's test rig is three overhead hooks on a circle 0.5 m from the bucket's axis, one above each
  ring, moved together; they hang 2.6 m above the floor when the rig carries. The grader's rig tests hold your
  robots' hooks the same way.
- **The steam**: the leak stops 3 s after the valve's lever reaches its closed stop.
- **The plume** (the plant's survey of where the steam is dangerous, a box round the leaking flange): 1.2 m along the
  pipe, centred on the flange; from 0.15 m behind the pipe's axis to 1.15 m in front of it, toward the bay; from 1.1 m
  to 3.2 m above the floor.
