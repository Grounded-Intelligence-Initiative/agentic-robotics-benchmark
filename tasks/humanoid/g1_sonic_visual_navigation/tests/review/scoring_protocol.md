# Timed aperture navigation calibration protocol

The verifier draws sixteen new courses after the participant is installed. Each has a stationary island between two physical, laterally sliding cabinet doors. At least one door can open fully. Eight courses have two full-stroke doors, four have only the left door capable of opening fully, and four have only the right door capable of opening fully. The other door in a single-service course moves but can expose at most 0.12 m of clear width. Controllers may use whichever aperture they can physically traverse.

Every panel includes eight left and eight right pivots. Within each pivot direction four courses have two fully moving doors, two have a full left stroke only and two have a full right stroke only. Eight courses have only the timed doorway. The remaining eight have an additional fixed cabinet opening, four to the left and four to the right. Each pivot direction has the same distribution. A random diagonal balances the side of the additional opening across the single-service families. The near or far position of the initial blue stopping waypoint, pivot amplitude and final walk length are independent stratified draws in the limits in the grader configuration. Initial robot state, cabinet geometry and both initial shutter phases use separate verifier-owned random inputs. The generator defines the two right and left phase offsets from the independent phase seed. The sixteen episodes run in a fresh shuffled order.

## What completes a course

The controller receives only the current native RGB-D observation and robot-local sensors. The verifier asks once per decision and applies only the returned body codes. Each applied row advances ten 2 ms native intervals. A course contributes a single success when all the following conditions hold within one reset.

- The pelvis crosses the moving-leaf plane forward through the geometrically exposed part of either physical aperture. The private verifier reconstructs both lateral bounds from the measured slide position at the interpolated crossing time, the fixed aperture edges and the 1.08 m leaf width. It independently checks the reported exposed width, projected crossing coordinates and strictly positive nearer-edge clearance. It then requires a later crossing of the fixed cabinet midplane through that same side. The moving plane is 0.14 m in front of the frame.
- Any extra fixed cabinet is crossed through its actual opening in order. Interpolated crossing points are checked at their physical planes. Pelvis-edge clearances describe the crossing point. Native contacts check the rest of the robot on both sides of each crossing, including during shutter closing.
- Throughout the course every 2 ms interval must be free of robot contact with doors, jambs, the island and additional cabinets. The approach must stay inside the allowed lateral course boundary. The approach, signed pivot and departure must each attain their native upright, supported quiet stop. The first such stop includes its additional dwell. Falls, collisions, missed passages, lost holds, time limits and aborted controllers remain in the denominator.

The raw score equals strict physical successes divided by sixteen. Neither the service family, chosen side, waiting time, code changes nor partial stage completion contributes points. Clearance through the actual opening determines validity. Time to the crossing and time spent at ≤0.10 m/s planar speed in the metre before the moving plane are reported alongside action cost, native contact counts and stop records. The scorer reports actual 50 Hz body rows, 64 FSQ scalars per applied row, within-episode coordinate changes and pooled 29-joint acceleration from measured velocities spaced 0.10 s apart.

## Prospective numerical rule

Freeze the physical environment, submitted reference, grader and roster algorithm before opening two independent official measurements with the anchor unset. Both must achieve at least fifteen of sixteen strict courses, both pivot directions and each door-service family must have a strict success, and there must be zero solver aborts. A deficient reference is retained in the measurement record. Further calibration follows a substantive revision with a new prospective registration.

The bootstrap's measured strict fraction sets the anchor. The lower of the two qualifying physical success counts determines how many completions reach full normalized credit. If both achieve sixteen, full credit requires sixteen. If either achieves fifteen, full credit requires fifteen. Keep both raw counts and calibrate the saturation ratio as this required count divided by bootstrap successes. Two new ordinary ALE validations on the final numeric task must each report untouched zero and submitted reference one.
