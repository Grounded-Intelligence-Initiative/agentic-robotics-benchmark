# Tools & Supplies

> Get ready before your humanoid robot arrives. Find the required tools and supplies, optional extras, and product links to help you prepare for assembly.

- Section: Humanoid › Overview

These are the tools you'll need to assemble a humanoid robot, along with some optional extras. Use this guide to prepare your workbench, see what each tool is for, and find the products we use ourselves.

The linked products are references, not a requirement to buy a particular brand. None of these links are affiliate links or advertisements; they're simply what we use, so feel free to choose suitable tools from your favourite brand instead.

## Required Tools and Supplies

These tools and supplies are needed for standard assembly, including the wire joins required at certain actuator joints and basic electrical checks.

### Metric Allen keys

_Included within your humanoid robot purchase_

Used for most of the robot's assembly. Make sure you have 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, and 6 mm keys available.

**As a humanoid robot customer, you do not need to buy this.** A metric Allen-key set is included free with your humanoid robot order or preorder and arrives with your robot delivery.

Product: SATA ST09118 9-piece T-handle hex key set



### Phillips screwdriver

Used to secure the battery switch to the battery casing. Choose a Phillips bit that fits the screw head correctly.

Product: Klein Tools 32717 precision screwdriver set



### Flush cutters

Used to trim wire ends or small parts where an assembly step calls for cutting. Check the cutter's material and wire-size rating before use.

Product: Pro'sKit PM-805E 135 mm plastic cutting pliers



### Wire strippers

Used to remove insulation from wires before connecting actuators at certain joints.

Product: VESSEL No.3000A wire stripper



### Heat gun

Used to shrink heat-shrink tubing over completed wire joins. Control the heat and keep it away from nearby plastics and other heat-sensitive parts.

Product: STANLEY STEL670-XD 2000W heat gun



### Soldering iron

Used with solder to join wire ends at the joint locations identified in the assembly instructions. Use a suitable tip and temperature for the wire and solder.

Product: QUECOO T12-956 soldering station



### Soldering third-hand clamp

Holds wire ends steady while you solder them together. Position the clamps so they do not damage the insulation or strain the wire.

Product: KOTTO four-arm third-hand soldering tool



### Digital multimeter

Needed for continuity checks after making wire connections to confirm the intended electrical paths are connected. Also used for voltage checks when debugging the electrical system. Perform continuity checks only with the battery disconnected and the circuit unpowered.

Product: UNI-T UT60 series digital multimeters



### Solder

Used with the soldering iron to make the documented wire joins. Lead-free solder still requires suitable fume control.

Product: Asahi CLF5013 SAC305 lead-free solder wire



### Heat-shrink tubing

Insulates soldered wire joins. Select tubing that fits over the join and shrinks to a secure fit; slide it onto the wire before soldering when the join would prevent fitting it afterward.

Product: 3M FP-301 black assorted heat-shrink tubing kit



### Threadlocker

Helps prevent specified bolts from loosening after assembly. Apply to the threads as directed before tightening; do not substitute a high-strength grade or apply it to every fastener indiscriminately.

Product: LOCTITE 242 medium-strength threadlocker



### Super glue

Secures wires to metal at specified routing locations to help prevent pinching. Check material compatibility and keep adhesive away from connectors and moving joints.

Product: LOCTITE 401 instant adhesive



### Masking / paper tape

Used for temporary labels, notes, and holding parts, wires, nuts, or bolts in place during assembly. It is not a substitute for electrical insulation or permanent wire retention.

Product: NICHIBAN Washi Tape No.210



## Optional Tools and Workshop Equipment

These items can make assembly or troubleshooting easier, but you do not need to buy them for standard assembly. This section also includes optional teleoperation equipment and development and prototyping equipment that we use in our workshop; customers do not need any of it to assemble the robot.

### The gantry crane

Our open-source crane for lifting and positioning the robot. Build guide, BOM, and design files are on GitHub.

Product: maker/gantry-crane on GitHub

### Open Stool

Our open reference design for a sturdy support stool to sit on or position the robot during setup and maintenance.

**We will provide the GitHub for this soon.**

### Soft-face mallet

Can help gently seat a tight-fitting part where the assembly instructions permit it. Check alignment and obstructions first; do not force a defective part or strike an actuator, bearing, or electronics.

Product: Estwing DFH12 rubber mallet hammer



### Powered cordless screwdriver

Speeds up repetitive tightening and loosening instead of turning every fastener manually. Start threads by hand and use the final-tightening method specified in the assembly step to avoid cross-threading or over-tightening.

**Warning: a cordless screwdriver increases the risk of stripping bolt heads or threads.** Avoid using one unless you are experienced with powered screwdrivers and torque control. Use hand tools if you are unsure.

Product: Bosch GO Gen 3 cordless screwdriver



### Thermal camera

Used to visually locate unusually warm areas when investigating possible overheating. It does not replace the robot's temperature monitoring or safety checks.

Product: HABOTEST HT821A/B series thermal imaging thermometer



### Desktop soldering fume absorber

Helps capture fumes near the soldering work area. This is the desktop unit we use; an appropriate existing extraction setup may serve instead.

Follow the manufacturer's positioning and filter-maintenance instructions, and provide suitable ventilation.

Product: HAKKO FA400 desktop solder fume absorbing unit



### VR headset (optional)

Used with the PICO Motion Tracker for teleoperation and control of the humanoid robot. This headset is optional and is not required to assemble the robot.

Product: PICO 4



### Motion tracker (optional)

Used with the PICO 4 headset for teleoperation and control of the humanoid robot. This tracker is optional and is not required to assemble the robot.

Product: PICO Motion Tracker



### PCB heating platform

Used for PCB heating during electronics development and prototyping.

Product: iTECH digital hot plate soldering station



### Brushless drill

A workshop tool for fabrication and prototyping.

Product: DCA ADJZ05-13 brushless drill



### Desktop CNC machine

Used to machine parts for development and prototyping.

Product: Makera Z1 desktop CNC



### Digital oscilloscope

Used to inspect electrical signals during advanced electronics debugging and prototyping.

Product: RIGOL DHO800 series digital oscilloscope



### 3D printer

Used to make prototype parts and iterate on designs.

Product: Bambu Lab P1S 3D printer



### Mini digital clamp meter

Used in our workshop for battery inspection and advanced electrical diagnostics.

Capabilities vary across the linked series. Battery DC-current measurements require a DC-capable model.

Product: UNI-T UT210 series mini clamp meters
