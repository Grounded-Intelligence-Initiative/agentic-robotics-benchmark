CALIBRATION PROCEDURE: THE MAKER'S CALIBRATION VIDEO, WRITTEN OUT BY THE LAB

The maker's setup guide asks that its calibration video be watched in full before the actuators are calibrated.
The video itself is not kept with these records. This file is the lab's written account of what the video shows,
joint by joint and in the video's order of work. Actuators are named by their slot numbers and names (01 to 25).
The figures named below (fig01 to fig11, beside this file) show the parts that make each reference; see FIGURES
at the end.

1. BEFORE STARTING

- Calibration zeroes every actuator, so that the maker's walking controller runs correctly. It is done in the
  maker's app, Humanoid Manager, on a phone or a laptop; the maker recommends a laptop for the larger view.
- Starting position: the unit lies on its back on floor mats, both arms along its sides, both legs straight.
- The app's Actuators page has three tabs: Actuator ID, Liveness Check and Calibration. First the liveness
  check: all 25 actuators must answer and light up on the screen. Then the Calibration tab: confirm the warning
  and enable calibration.
- Left and right are the unit's own: facing the unit, its left side is on your right. The maker suggests a
  sticky note on each side so that the sides are not confused.
- In the video one person does every step: the dominant hand holds the limb, the other hand works the app.
- After each Calibrate the app shows a green tick for that actuator, and its reading is zero radians.

2. HIP PITCH: 01 L_hip_pitch, THEN 07 R_hip_pitch (figure 1)

With the hand under the calf, the leg is lifted and pushed toward the head until the steel tab of the hip-pitch
bracket meets the orange metal stop plate on the hip and locks against it. The leg is kept pushed and held there
by hand while Calibrate is clicked. When the app shows the actuator calibrated and reading zero radians, the leg
is lowered. The right leg is done the same way.

3. HIP ROLL: 02 L_hip_roll, THEN 08 R_hip_roll (figure 2)

Two M4 x 10 bolts serve as a temporary stop. A bolt is put by hand into the free hole on the inner, lower side
of the roll actuator's ring face. With the hand under the calf, the leg is lifted a little so that it moves
freely, then swung inward until the hip-roll bracket rests on the bolt head and locks there. It is held there
while Calibrate is clicked. The bolt is then taken out and used in the same way on the right roll actuator: the
right calf lifted a little, the leg swung inward onto the bolt head, Calibrate. When both hip rolls are done
the bolts are removed; the maker stresses not to forget them.

4. HIP YAW: 03 L_hip_yaw, THEN 09 R_hip_yaw (figure 3)

The yaw actuator sits inside the hip, out of sight; it turns the leg about the leg's own long axis. With the
hand under the calf, the leg is lifted a little and turned so that the toes go inward. On the way, the plastic
thigh cover catches on the metal of the roll actuator. The cover's edge is lifted over the metal with a
finger, and the leg is turned on as far as it goes, until the thigh cover bottoms out against the metal
hip-roll bracket. It is held there while Calibrate is clicked; then the leg may be turned back. The right leg
is done the same way, turned the other way round so that its toes go inward too.

5. KNEE: 04 L_knee, THEN 10 R_knee (figure 4)

The leg is lifted all the way up and the shank pushed until the knee locks out. The maker uses the lock-out
because the knee then always sits at the mechanical end of its travel. Calibrate is clicked. The right knee
is done the same way.

6. ANKLE: 05+06 L_ankle_A + L_ankle_B, THEN 11+12 R_ankle_A + R_ankle_B (figure 5)

The maker calls the ankle a very important part of the calibration: if it is not done correctly, the walking
controller will likely not work properly. The two ankle actuators of a foot sit on the shank, each driving the
foot through a crank and a bar. By turning the foot by hand, first one way and then the other, each crank in
turn is brought to where its metal piece sits exactly between two screws. The foot is then turned back to its
neutral position, where it looks very straight and flat; the maker gives this as the sign that the step was
done right. Both actuators of the foot are calibrated together with Calibrate Both. The right foot is done the
same way.

7. WAIST: 23 waist_yaw (figure 6)

The metal part above the waist and the metal part below it are brought into line, fully aligned, by eye.
Calibrate is clicked. This completes the lower body.

8. ARMS: 13 TO 17 LEFT, THEN 18 TO 22 RIGHT (figures 7, 8 and 9)

The maker calls the upper body much simpler than the lower body. Both arms are taken in hand.
- First a check on each shoulder: the face with the circle of six bolts (the front face of HM1_400_01A, where it
  is bolted on the shoulder-roll actuator; right: HM1_300_01A) must face toward you. If the smooth face (the
  back face of HM1_400_03A; right: HM1_300_03A) shows instead, the arm is in the wrong orientation and is turned
  until the six-bolt face shows. This must be right on both sides.
- Left arm: the arm is turned outward about its own axis as far as it goes, until it cannot turn any further
  (figure 7). The elbow is bent up as far as it goes, so that the arm folds like a wing (figure 9). The arm is
  laid down on the floor beside the body and pushed inward all the way, until the upper arm meets the torso at
  the armpit (figure 8).
- Right arm: the same, turned the other way round (outward on its own side), elbow bent up, laid down on the
  floor, pushed in against the armpit.
- As a final check, the wrist and the elbow of each arm are pressed down so that the arm stays in this
  position.
- The left arm's actuators are then calibrated in turn, from this one position: 13 L_shoulder_pitch,
  14 L_shoulder_roll, 15 L_shoulder_yaw, 16 L_elbow, 17 L_wrist_yaw. Then the right arm's: 18 R_shoulder_pitch,
  19 R_shoulder_roll, 20 R_shoulder_yaw, 21 R_elbow, 22 R_wrist_yaw. The video names no reference for the
  wrist yaw: it is calibrated as it lies.
- On the app's view both arms now show green.

9. NECK: 24 neck_yaw AND 25 neck_pitch (figures 10 and 11)

With one hand behind the head, the head is pushed all the way up, chin toward the chest, as far as it goes
(figure 11). Held there, the head is set so that the vertical seam on the back of the head runs straight down
the middle and divides the part below it in half (figure 10). Still holding, Calibrate is clicked on
24 neck_yaw, then on 25 neck_pitch. All 25 actuators now show green.

10. CHECK CALIBRATION

Before the check the unit is put back in its starting position: the head let down to rest all the way; the legs
spread a little, roughly parallel to each other; on each side the front thigh cover (HM1_600_04C; right:
HM1_500_04C) centred on the middle of the metal hip-roll bracket. Check Calibration is then clicked: every
actuator should show a green tick, and the calibration is complete. The maker also shows what a wrong position
looks like: with one hip yaw turned all the way out, the check reports many errors; with the leg turned back to
the middle, and the ankles set right, the check shows all green again. After this the unit can be stood up and
driven.
